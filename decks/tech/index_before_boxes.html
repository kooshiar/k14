Each design round has one job

1. Build

An AI agent assembles candidates from proven binding parts: a grabbing group (urea, squaramide, guanidinium), arms, and linkers. 80% are new combinations, 20% are known compounds (papers, PubChem) so we can compare against today’s options.

Example

Selenium catcher: 1,008 new designs + 180 known compounds = 1,188 candidates.

2. Screen

Each candidate meets the target and each look-alike in 6 random poses, in implicit water. A fast force field (GFN-FF) relaxes every pose. The best 100 designs plus the 10 best known compounds move on.

Example

Selenium catcher: every binder is tried with selenate, sulfate and bicarbonate; 1,188 → 110.

In → out1,200 → 110
Machine96 CPU cores
Time5 min
Work~30,000 relaxations

3. Refine

A semi-quantum method (GFN2-xTB) re-relaxes the best 4 poses per molecule. It handles charges and hydrogen bonds much better than the fast screen.

Example

Selenium catcher: squaramide binders rise to the top; resin-like ammonium groups drop because they bind sulfate just as well.

In → out110 → 23
Machine96 CPU cores
Time15–30 min
Work~1,800 relaxations

4. Score

UMA (AI trained on 100M quantum calculations) gives near-DFT energies. We add the cost of leaving water and the loss of motion (entropy), so the result is binding free energy, the number labs measure. Selectivity = target vs look-alikes, weighted by how much more of each is in the water.

Example

Good binder in water: about −5 kcal/mol or stronger, and ≥2 kcal/mol better on the target than on the look-alike.

In → out23 → ranked
MachineGPU + CPU
Time~15 min
Work~100 UMA calls

5. Shake

Each top binder holding the target goes into a box of real water molecules (~600–1,400 atoms). UMA computes the forces every 0.5 fs for 2,000 steps (1 ps). If the target drifts away, the binder fails.

Example

Selenium catcher: all top 3 kept selenate bound for the full run.

Time5–13 min each
Work2,000 MD steps

Round 1 · Explore

Builds ~1,200 candidates per problem (80% new combinations of proven binding parts, 20% known compounds) and runs the full funnel: fast screen, quantum-level check, free energy, water MD.

Example

Formaldehyde: 217 candidates; 2-methylpiperazine beats urea.

In → out~1,200 → top 20
Time1–2 h per problem

Round 2 · Improve

Takes our top 4 designs and the top 2 products sold today, makes small changes that keep them easy to make (add a group, swap O for S, add a methyl, change an arm), and scores the best 60.

Example

Cement: the level-2 pick reaches 90 of 100 vs 56 for the best product sold today.

In → out~1,600 variants → 60 scored
Time20–60 min per problem

Round 3 · US-sourced

Only for designs that contain atoms the US mostly imports (fluorine: fluorspar 100% imported; iodine). Swaps them for chlorine, bromine, methyl or hydrogen and re-scores, so the product can be made from US raw materials.

Example

Selenium catcher: 7 squaramide designs with CF₃ arms get chlorine versions.

In → outdesigns with F or I → ≤40 scored
Time~30 min per problem

Round 4 · Cheaper

Rebuilds the best designs from cheaper building blocks and shorter synthesis routes, then keeps only those that lose under 1 kcal/mol of binding.
